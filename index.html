POgrasp

Shape belief for active reconstruction & dexterous grasping

October 2026

A single view leaves part of an object’s shape unknown. POgrasp studies how a robot hand can use contact to update a belief over possible shapes and choose a grasp.

The current work has established a grasping and evaluation pipeline and tested when a policy uses shape information. Connecting a visual prior to contact-based belief updates is the next stage.

Grasping experiments

The experiments use a simulated Tesollo DG-5F hand with a floating wrist. Policies receive state-based observations, including privileged object information. The videos show selected rollouts on training objects.

Limits found in the pilots

An initial estimator using contact and proprioceptive history did not reliably replace the handle-direction input. It also used privileged object displacement and shape summaries. The tested histories did not provide enough useful information for the estimator-driven policy.

Later evaluations and experiment references

A later two-object policy’s final checkpoint achieved 30/32 on the open cup and 25/32 on the lidded cup. Zeroing handle direction reduced the latter to 9/32.

The history-estimator experiments produced 2–7/32 lidded-cup successes across recorded reviews. At the last reviewed checkpoint, estimator input gave 5/32 and true-direction input gave 11/32. The policy had also changed during adaptation, so these counts cannot be directly compared with the earlier 25/32.

The results cover small trained object sets. Evaluation on unseen shapes and comparisons with published methods remain to be done. Experiment records and video hashes.

Next steps

The proposed shape belief will retain multiple plausible geometries while preserving details relevant to grasping. One representation question is whether an SO(3)- or SE(3)-equivariant encoder can keep intrinsic shape distinguishable from pose without losing reconstruction quality.

We will connect the visual prior to contact updates and test whether the policy gathers and uses useful information. Comparisons will cover vision, contact and their combination, with repeated training seeds and new objects. Improvements and negative findings will both be reported.

Code and reproducibility materials are planned for release after paper publication.
